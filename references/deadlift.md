# Deadlift analysis rules

## Evidence model

- Bar: visible hub center. Use lift-off x as the vertical reference.
- Hip and shoulder: consistent lateral estimates. Torso angle comes from the shoulder-to-hip segment.
- Bar metric: `max(abs(x - lift_off_x)) / plate_diameter * 100`. Heuristic labels: ≤5% close to vertical, >5–10% mild screen drift, >10% visible screen drift.

Describe direction and phase, not only a score. A bar that moves toward the lifter and returns can form an S-shaped screen path; this is not a danger verdict by itself.

## Early hip rise

Call visible pre-lift hip rise only when the bar stays near its ground baseline (about ±3 px), the hip rises beyond landmark uncertainty (about 10 px), and shoulder motion or torso-angle change supports it. Normal hip extension after lift-off is not “butt shooting up.”

Possible contributors: overly squat-like start, knees too far forward, low hamstring pre-tension, delayed brace, lat tension, or slack pull. Heavy loads amplify setup inefficiency. Do not reduce the finding to “weak legs” without corroborating evidence.

Also check midfoot setup, bar closeness, whole-foot pressure, lumbar and neck stability, shoulder–hip synchronization, and lockout without backward hyperextension.
